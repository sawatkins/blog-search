# Varied-blog backfill pilot — 2026-09-07

## Result

Ran 985 crawl jobs across six blogs in three bounded passes (406, 396, and 183).
The passes were stopped/restarted to load fixes, not run concurrently. Finished
at 02:51:28 UTC with no active leases, immediately due work, or pending index writes
within the pilot's discovery budgets. Daily scheduling remains off.

PostgreSQL and Elasticsearch both increased from 314,167 to 314,974 documents:
807 net-new pages, plus 24 existing pages refreshed. All 831 retained pages touched
by the pilot matched Elasticsearch on title, URL, text, and publication date.
All 314,167 preexisting page IDs remain in PostgreSQL.

These are searchable-page counts, not a claim that every page is an individual
blog post. Recognizable navigation listings are excluded; unusual static or index
pages can still have extractable content and need further quality evaluation.

## Fixes exposed by the test

- Historical discovery previously stopped at HTML pages with unfamiliar archive
  names. Historical pages now yield in-scope links, even when article extraction
  finds no text. This opened additional Tim Bray posts, for example
  `/ongoing/When/202x/2024/12/12/Quamina-Regular-Expression-Series` at HTML depth two.
  Fresh feed posts retain their extraction-only fast path; depth, domain, robots,
  and database discovery budgets still apply.
- Increasing a backfill budget now rechecks historical pages as well as known
  archives/sitemaps, clearing HTTP validators so it can rediscover omitted links.
  It preserves running leases and leaves fresh entries and other blogs alone.
- Sitemaps incorrectly treated recognizable home/category/post-list URLs as
  articles. Sitemap and HTML discovery now share listing classification; existing
  historical page jobs also recognize these listings without indexing their text.
  A real article ending in `/2002/post/` remains a page, as do query-based posts.
- Sitemap nesting no longer consumes the separate HTML traversal depth budget.

All 148 regression tests passed against disposable PostgreSQL 17. Unit-file
validation and `git diff --check` also passed.

## Limits, normal failures, and cleanup

Historical-job budgets remain 100 each for Dave Rupert, Julia Evans, Simon
Willison, and Baldur Bjarnason; Matt Mullenweg and Tim Bray were increased from
100 to 200 to verify continuation. All six reached their caps (800 historical jobs
admitted in total). Fresh feed posts are exempt. Zero due jobs means the bounded
sample finished, not that these blogs' entire histories were crawled.

Three unavailable URLs were retained as terminal failures without stalling work:
one robots-blocked photo redirect, one broken historical link, and a guessed
`/sitemap.xml` returning 404, all on tbray.org. Three superseded page jobs remain
marked `archive_listing`; their archive work is represented separately.

Exactly 13 navigation documents created by this pilot were removed from both
PostgreSQL and Elasticsearch. Their discovery jobs were retained/reclassified,
their data was backed up, and none of the deleted document IDs reappeared after
the corrected sitemap was checked again.

Local backups, ignored by Git, are in
`/home/debian/blog-search/backup/backfill-pilot.bTOC1s/`:

- `blog-pages-before.csv`: 281 pre-pilot rows from the six blogs, including
  `original_url`; this is a scoped backup, not a complete database backup.
- `removed-pilot-listings.csv`: the 13 removed navigation documents.
- `feeds.txt`: the four added feed URLs.

The disposable test database was removed after testing; backup files remain.
The live web service stayed active and was not restarted. No timers were installed,
no full-list import or full reindex ran, and SQS was untouched.
